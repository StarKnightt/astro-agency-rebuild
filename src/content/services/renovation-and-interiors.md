The most sustainable building is often the one that already exists. A lot of Norwegian houses from the twentieth century have good bones: solid structures, well-proportioned rooms and plots with mature gardens. What they need is better insulation, better daylight and a plan that fits how people live today.

We start every renovation with a proper survey of what is there. Measured drawings, moisture readings and a look behind a few wall panels tell us where the risks are before the budget is set.

Interiors are part of the architecture, not something added at the end. We design kitchens, stairs, built-in storage and lighting together with the rooms, using a small palette of materials that age well: oak, lime plaster, linoleum, wool and stone.
